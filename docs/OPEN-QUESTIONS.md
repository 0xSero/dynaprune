# OPEN QUESTIONS — ranked by risk to the method

Ranked by how much a bad answer would invalidate published points. Each carries the
stage where it would surface and what would settle it.

| # | Question | Why it matters | Surfaces at | Settled by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does H1 hold at all? On a calibrated base, does non-uniform compensated pruning beat equal-byte demotion, or does the instance result (61.8% vs 77.4% top-1) generalize? | The entire "dynamic" slider is only useful if some pruned higher-tier point is `recommended`. If demotion wins everywhere, the tool is a measurement device that says so | S4 vs `equal_byte_demotion` | The first full frontier with all four arms at two tiers |
| 2 | Is the true admission ceiling on the serving node stable across engine builds? | The byte budget that picks the prune fraction comes from a measured ceiling. If it moves with the image digest, points fit on one build and OOM on the next | S3 | A ceiling receipt per image digest; `policy.target.reserve_bytes` sized from the worst observed |
| 3 | Are observation panels and evaluation panels actually independent enough? | Routing mass captured on text that resembles the evaluation panel leaks selection signal; the hash inequality check catches identity, not similarity | S0, S7 | A held-out panel from a different domain scored once per `recommended` point as a spot check |
| 4 | How much does STEP-style compensation need the full `mean_output` vectors versus norms only? | The observation schema allows norms in-line and vectors in a sidecar. If norms are insufficient, providers must ship vectors (hundreds of MB) and H3 tests get expensive | S1, S4 | H3 arms with and without vectors on one tier |
| 5 | Which per-layer rate rule wins: global greedy rank, write-gain proportional, or explicit clamps? | H2. The instance ledger used greedy with per-layer clamps; the write-gain spread argues for a formula. Different rules can give the same total bytes and very different tails | S4 `kl_p90` | H2 arms at equal bytes |
| 6 | Is the byte census from safetensors headers exact for every packed format? | The solver promises byte-exact accounting. Formats with per-tensor metadata, scales or alignment padding could make `expected_total ≠ actual_total` by small amounts | S2 | Test fixtures for each supported packer; a tolerance policy of zero unless a format proves otherwise |
| 7 | What does "N cold runs agree" tolerate on speed cells? | Decode throughput on a shared unified-memory device drifts with thermals; too tight a tolerance rejects good points, too loose hides regressions | S6 | Repeat-spread statistics from the anchor point across a week |
| 8 | Do engine loaders accept non-uniform per-layer unit counts without patches? | The SGLang path was proven on the instance overlay; vLLM was not. If a loader pads or masks, the "never masking" invariant breaks silently | S3 | Adapter `requirements()` probed on each engine version; a loader test that asserts tensor shapes per layer |
| 9 | Should task metrics ever become selection inputs? | H4 says audiences differ. Today task metrics are recorded but never select; promoting them means a second frontier and a second set of arms | §6 decision rules | A `task_metrics` frontier run once and compared with the KL frontier |
| 10 | How far do the plugin protocols stretch to heads and channels? | Heads couple q/k/v/o and GQA groups; channels couple up/gate/down. `bytes_per_unit` as a scalar may be wrong when units are not interchangeable | S1, S2 | A synthetic-model test for `attention_head` before any claim of support |
| 11 | Speculative decoding on pruned artifacts: does the draft path survive router surgery? | Draft layers may carry their own routed units that the plan protects; their interaction with a pruned main model is unmeasured | S3, S6 | Speed cells with and without the draft; a protected-layer check in S2 |
| 12 | Where do mirrored weights live, and are they ever authoritative? | The design says plans are the artifact and weights are a cache. Users will want to download weights; a mirror that drifts from the manifest is a silent fork | publish | Mirrors must carry the manifest and pass the same hash check on import; no other rule |
